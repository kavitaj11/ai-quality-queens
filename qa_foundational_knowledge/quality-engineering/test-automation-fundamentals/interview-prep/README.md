# Interview prep

Practice explaining a testing decision from your own experience. Bring one project example you can discuss without sharing confidential details.

## Study sequence

1. [Behavioral and hypothetical interview questions](behavioral-and-hypothetical-interview-questions.md) — rehearse a cross-team decision, a missed deadline or a professional goal. Identify what you personally decided and what you learned.
2. [Questions to ask the interviewer](questions-to-ask-the-interviewer.md) — invite the team to describe a recent release decision. Use the answer to ask who reviewed the evidence and how the team handled uncertainty.

## Worked example

Suppose a regression suite missed its release window. “We improved automation” leaves the listener guessing. Describe which checks were delayed, how you chose what to run first, and what risk the team accepted. Use a measured outcome when you have one. If you lack a baseline, explain how you would collect it.

## Rehearse one decision under follow-up questions

Choose a project you can discuss and prepare four notes: the risk, your personal responsibility, the choice you made and what you observed afterward. Keep team achievements and individual contributions clear. Use synthetic details for practice and use your own evidence in an interview.

| Interviewer prompt | What makes an answer assessable |
|---|---|
| Why did you automate that check first? | Identify the failure it detects, who would be affected and why that layer gives reliable evidence. |
| What did you defer? | Name an omitted check, its remaining risk and how you communicated it. |
| How do you know your change helped? | Give the observation, its comparison point and any uncertainty. |
| What would change your decision? | Identify a new constraint or piece of evidence and explain its effect. |

## Try a general QA scenario

A team has 20 minutes before a release review and its full suite takes 45. A credible answer begins by asking what changed, which checks exercise it and whether a recent run covers the same build. Explain how you would choose a subset, report untested areas and identify who owns the release decision. Several choices can be reasonable when the assumptions are explicit.

Reverse the conversation with a question of your own: “Can you walk me through a recent release where the team had incomplete test results?” Follow up on who interpreted the evidence and what happened after release.

## Practice assignment

Record a two-minute answer about a real trade-off. Ask a peer to identify the risk, your decision and the evidence for the outcome. Revise whichever point they could not recover. Finish by writing a question that would help you understand how the interviewing team handles a similar situation.
